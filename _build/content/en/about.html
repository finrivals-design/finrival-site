Why Finrival exists

Most market tools are built for people who already trade every day. They are full of tickers, jargon and numbers with no explanation. Finrival starts from the other end: the curious person who wants to understand what moved today, why it might have moved, and how two companies really compare, without being pushed to buy or sell anything.

Everything in Finrival is designed around three habits: research what is happening, compare what matters, and practise decisions with fictional money before real money is ever involved.

What Finrival is

  • The Finrival app for iPhone, with Android coming soon: Market, News, Compare, Rival and Watchlist in one place.
  • The Web Terminal for laptops and PCs: the same account and data on a larger screen, with live charts.
  • Finrival Learn on this site: free, plain-language guides to how markets and companies work.
  • Finrival AI: an assistant that explains prices, news and comparisons using Finrival's own stored numbers.
  • Rival: a practice portfolio with 100,000 fictional Rival Credits. They have no cash value and cannot be withdrawn.

Where the numbers come from

Finrival only shows data from licensed providers and says on every chart whether a number is stored or live:

  • Marketstack provides end-of-day prices and daily history for the stocks and ETFs in the Finrival catalogue. These closing prices are what Top Gainers, Top Decliners, Daily Trading Activity and Compare are built on.
  • API Ninjas provides intraday prices for live charts. These are shown to you and then discarded; Finrival does not keep a copy.
  • NewsData.io provides the news feed. Every story links to its original source.

Market data can be delayed or occasionally wrong. If something looks off, check it with an official source and tell us.

How Finrival AI is kept honest

Finrival AI uses a large language model, but it does not answer from memory alone. Before it replies, Finrival adds its own stored numbers for the company you ask about: the latest close, recent range and trend. Answers are checked by safety rules that remove anything that reads like a buy, sell or price-target recommendation, in all three languages.

AI can still make mistakes. Treat its answers as a starting point for your own research, never as a decision.
