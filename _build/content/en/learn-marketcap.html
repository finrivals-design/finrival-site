The formula

Market capitalisation, usually shortened to market cap, is the total value of all of a company's shares:

Market cap = share price × number of shares outstanding

If a company has 500 million shares and each trades at $40, its market cap is 500 million × $40 = $20 billion. That is the price the market as a whole puts on the company at this moment.

Why share price on its own is misleading

New investors often think a $300 share is "expensive" and a $10 share is "cheap". The share price on its own does not tell you either. Compare two invented companies:

  • Company A: share price $300, 50 million shares. Market cap: $15 billion.
  • Company B: share price $10, 4 billion shares. Market cap: $40 billion.

Company B is valued at more than twice Company A, even though each of its shares costs thirty times less. The price of one share mostly reflects how many pieces the company was cut into.

This is also why a stock split changes nothing about a company's value. If a company splits each share into ten, the price per share drops to a tenth, the number of shares grows ten times, and the market cap stays the same.

Large, mid and small caps

Companies are often grouped by size. The exact lines vary, but a common rough guide is:

  • Mega and large cap: above about $10 billion. Well-known companies, usually more stable.
  • Mid cap: roughly $2 billion to $10 billion.
  • Small cap: below about $2 billion. Often younger companies whose prices can swing far more.

Smaller companies tend to move more in percentage terms. A small cap rising or falling 20% in a day is unusual for a giant but far from rare for a small company. That is worth remembering when you look at the day's biggest movers.

What market cap does not tell you

Market cap is the market's price, not a verdict. A large market cap does not mean a company is profitable, well run or a good investment, and a small one does not mean it is a bargain. Market cap also ignores debt and cash; professionals use a related measure, enterprise value, which adds debt and subtracts cash.

Because the share price changes all day, market cap changes all day too. A company's ranking among the largest can change from one week to the next.
