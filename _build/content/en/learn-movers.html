How the lists are made

After the US market closes, Finrival takes the closing price of every stock in its catalogue and compares it with the previous day's close. The percentage change is:

Change % = (today's close − previous close) ÷ previous close × 100

A stock that closed at $10.47 after closing at $8.62 the day before rose by about 21.5%. Ranking every company by this number gives the Top Gainers at one end and the Top Decliners at the other.

Percentages are used instead of dollar amounts so that companies of every size can be compared fairly. A $5 move is huge for a $10 stock and tiny for a $500 one.

Why some stocks move so much

The biggest daily movers usually have something specific behind them:

  • Earnings: a quarterly report much better or worse than expected.
  • Company news: a contract, a product launch, a regulatory decision, a takeover offer.
  • Sector news: a whole group, such as chip makers or space companies, moving together.
  • Size: smaller companies swing more in percentage terms, so they appear on these lists more often.

Finrival shows the news linked to a company next to its move, and Finrival AI can summarise what was reported. Sometimes there is no clear news at all, and that is worth knowing too.

What the lists do not tell you

  • They are not recommendations. Being on Top Gainers does not mean a stock is a good buy, and Top Decliners are not bargains by default.
  • They say nothing about tomorrow. Large moves often partly reverse, and sometimes continue. No one can tell reliably in advance.
  • One day is a small window. A stock up 20% today may still be down over the past year. Check the longer chart before drawing conclusions.

Movers and Daily Trading Activity

Next to the movers, Finrival shows Daily Trading Activity: how the day's trading value was spread across sectors such as technology, finance and industrials. A sector can attract a large share of trading even when its prices barely change, so the two views together give a better picture than either one alone.
